A **collection** holds documents and finds the ones closest in meaning to a
question: "How many vacation days?" finds "Annual leave is 25 days" without
sharing a word. Give it to an agent as a tool and the agent can look things
up before it answers.

## How it works

- Each document is embedded (turned into a vector by your embedding model)
  and stored in your storage with its text and metadata: SQLite, Postgres or
  memory, **encrypted when the storage is**, kept and deleted like any
  memory.
- When the collection opens, its vectors are read once and indexed in your
  process's memory; `add` and `remove` keep the index up to date. If another
  process adds documents, `reload()` picks them up.
- `search(query, k)` embeds the question and returns the best `k` hits
  (`id`, `text`, `metadata`, `score`); `where` keeps only documents whose
  metadata matches.
- `as_tool(name, description)` gives an agent a read-only search tool: the
  model sends a `query` (and optionally `k`, up to 20) and gets the hits,
  each text cut to 2 000 characters.
- In Rust, anything can be a `Retriever` (a web search, your database) and
  become a tool with `retriever_tool`.

## Which index

| | Flat (default) | HNSW (`index="hnsw"`) |
|---|---|---|
| Results | exact | approximate |
| Query at 100 000 documents | 17 ms | 0.2 ms |
| Recall (share of the exact top 10) | 1.0 | 0.88 to 1.0 on clustered data |
| Opening the collection | under a second | about 54 s at 100 000 documents |

Numbers from one machine, 768-dimension vectors (`docs/benchmarks.md`).
Keep the default unless a large collection is queried very often; raise
`ef_search` if HNSW misses results.

## Limits

Up to 100 000 characters of text per document (split longer ones), 8 KB of
metadata, ids up to 256 bytes, and 2 000 000 vectors per index. Documents are
embedded 64 at a time. Vectors from another embedding model are ignored, so
changing models means re-adding the documents.

Why not `pgvector` or `sqlite-vec`? Both keep vectors unencrypted in the
database, and an embedding can be turned back into text. CriogenAI keeps them
encrypted and searches in memory instead.
